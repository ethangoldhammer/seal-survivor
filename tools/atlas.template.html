Model Atlas

Every 3D model the game actually loads, measured at the size it renders. Sizes are fit × sizeMultiplier — the fit written in assets.js times the Look & Sound multiplier saved in imported-tuning.json, which is what the merged config produces at boot. Geometry, textures and clip names were read out of the binaries themselves, and the proportions come from Box3.setFromObject on the real three.js loaders — the same measurement assets.js uses to compute scale.

Changed 10 Aug 2026 The crab got a claw that opens. crabwalking.glb could not pinch at any price: every arm chain ended at a leaf ...ArmPalm bone and the claw head was one closed 424-vertex lump with no interior gap on any of its own three axes — a shut fist. Both crabs now load crabpincer.glb, whose wrist forks into two finger chains driving separate geometry; rotating the movable one takes the tip-to-tip aperture from 9% of finger length to 33%, a measured +277% on the bone and +356% in play. It is also cheaper than what it replaced: the download was 8.64 MB and 92,491 triangles — more than double the heaviest asset in this roster, on the one creature that spawns twenty at a time — so tools/optimize-crab.mjs welds, decimates to 13%, quantises and re-encodes the three 1024² maps to WebP, landing at 1.09 MB and 12,012 triangles against the old crab's 2.74 MB and 8,520. The script re-measures the two finger chains afterwards and fails if the simplifier ate them, because a claw that has lost its prongs looks perfectly fine standing still and simply never opens. The one thing that did get dearer is the skeleton: 126 bones against 42. crabwalking.glb was left unreferenced in public/ for a fortnight after that, which is not free — vite copies the folder wholesale, so it kept shipping 2.87 MB to every player. What they actually look like

Every model rendered from its own binary with the three.js version the game ships, lit the same way and framed the same way: the asset's declared forward points right and its up points up, so the boats and the moray come out rotated here exactly as they are rotated in play. Anything with an animation is posed a third of the way into its swim clip rather than shown in bind pose — a skinned creature standing in its rest pose reads as a specimen, not an animal. Sized to fill the frame, so these show shape and finish, not scale; scale is the next two sections.

Everything, to scale

drag / scroll sideways

Each rectangle is one asset drawn at its true rendered size: length along its declared forward axis, height along its up axis, sharing one world-unit ruler. Fill depth encodes size band. The three boats and the moray sit rotated relative to the swimmers — their forward/up pair is deliberately mapped to the side-view frame, so their long axis reads as height here.

The size ledger

Sorted largest first. World is the longest axis after scaling — the number the game hands to createVisual(). Hitbox is the enemy's config radius times the same multiplier, which is how enemies.js derives it, so it tracks the body you actually see.

Plates

one per asset

Texture swatches are the base-colour maps lifted out of the .glb binaries. Where a plate says untextured, the file ships maps the loader cannot reach — noted per plate and explained in the findings.

Loose texture library

public/textures · public/sprites

Files layered on top of a model rather than shipped inside one, wired in assets.js via texture: { emissive }. Two kinds, and the difference matters: a two-tone mask is white markings on black, so only the markings emit — what CONFIG.glow describes. A composite is the creature's whole base texture with its brightest region blown to white, so the entire body emits. Ten are composites left over from the tool's default mode; the three generated from external source art are two-tone.

Procedural assets

no model file

The other 13 entries in ASSETS are built from code — displaced icospheres, primitives, and one sprite pool. They carry size multipliers too, so they belong in the same ledger.

What the survey turned up

Nothing here is broken gameplay — most of it is weight and drift. Ordered by how much it costs.

How this was measured. Asset definitions and the size multipliers come from bundling path/src/assets.js and path/src/config.js with esbuild and reading the merged CONFIG — so every number reflects imported-tuning.json deep-merged over the config.js defaults, not the source literals. Triangles, bones, embedded images and clip names were parsed straight out of the GLB/FBX binaries. Bounding proportions come from running the real GLTFLoader and FBXLoader under Node and calling Box3.setFromObject, which is what assets.js itself does to compute fit.

The renders. Made by loading each binary with the project's own three.js under real WebGL, sampling its swim clip at 33% so skinned creatures are posed rather than in bind pose, and framing on the declared forward/up pair so every asset is shown in the frame the game shows it in. Cropped to the alpha bounds, so each keeps its own aspect ratio. They are lit by one fixed three-light rig, which means finish and colour are comparable between plates but none of this is the game's own shading — no modelUnlit, no glow, no outline, no water.

Read-only. Nothing in public/ was modified except models/hammerhead.glb, which was rebuilt from its source to restore the UVs it had been shipped without. No tuning save was triggered.
